# ICP & User Priority  
- **Life-science/Pharma Executives (CMO/Head of Commercial)** – *Buyer & Sponsor.*  These leaders set strategy, budgets and KPIs, so their main concern is ROI, speed, and regulatory risk, not daily use.  In fact, ~70–85% of top pharma leaders now see AI as an “immediate priority” and are boosting AI budgets.  They expect tools to accelerate campaign execution (e.g. faster approvals or content reuse) and squeeze costs, but are cautious about compliance and complexity.  Only ~10–15% of execs feel “very comfortable” with GenAI today, so SwishX must prove business value (e.g. “we halve content production time”) with minimal friction.  **Role:** Decision-maker/buyer; sets requirements (compliance, metrics) but will not be primary content-creator. They will validate ROI and insist on enterprise governance (e.g. integration with PromoMats/Veeva).  

- **Marketing Teams and Managers** – *Primary Users & Project Leads.*  These are the people who actually *need* new assets.  They define briefs, choose channels (email, HCP presentations, web, social), and coordinate agencies/designers.  Marketing leads rely on traditional tools (Office, email, slide decks) to drive campaigns.  In one pharma marketing role, “expert in Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, Excel” was required, underscoring how central PowerPoint and Word are to their workflow.  They also work in Veeva PromoMats to store and route content.  Marketers feel a huge pain from slow content pipelines: for example, transforming a scientific slide deck or whitepaper into campaign visuals typically takes agencies many weeks.  They value tools that cut this time (AI image generators are already popular for rapid mockups).  **Role:** Champion and day‑to‑day user of SwishX.  They seek quick drafts and scalable variations (to A/B-test or localize), but need final output to be compliant.  They influence feature prioritization (e.g. brand templates, MLR-safe prompts) and ultimately decide adoption based on productivity gains.  

- **In-House Design Teams/Creative Leads** – *Expert Validators & Influencers.*  These are the internal art directors, graphic designers, and creative services managers.  They are often the “trusted experts” who review all agency work and (in some companies) handle smaller design projects.  They have high technical skills – for example, a Head of Creative is expected to have “expert level skill in Adobe Creative Suite (Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign) [and] experience in Adobe XD, Figma, and PowerPoint”.  They care deeply about brand consistency, visual quality and compliance (color specs, FDA-required content).  In practice, marketing relies on them to catch problems and polish SwishX drafts.  **Role:** Influencers/validators.  They will demand SwishX output meets design standards.  They may not be the main operator, but SwishX must output in formats (vectors, layers, editable designs) they trust.  If SwishX threatens their domain (e.g. poor art direction), they can block adoption.  On the other hand, they can become advocates if SwishX markedly simplifies repetitive edits (e.g. resizing, copying style across images).  

- **Specialist Pharma/Life-Sciences Agencies** – *Power Users & Quality Benchmarks.*  These niche agencies (e.g. medical communications firms, healthcare advertisers) produce much of the industry’s high-end content (animations, videos, complex infographics).  They are very comfortable with creative tools and increasingly with AI: for instance, IPG Health’s chief operator reports using AI to align briefs and check compliance.  Agencies remain “essential strategic partners” – AI is automating the grunt work so they “focus on higher-value activities such as campaign strategy, brand positioning, audience research, [and] creative direction”.  In effect, they set the quality bar (they will scrutinize SwishX content) but also consume great volume.  **Role:** Power users/early adopters.  They could use SwishX to prototype drafts or bulk-generate variants before applying their expertise.  Their endorsement would lend credibility.  Differences: agencies juggle many clients/brands (and global markets), so they need SwishX to handle multi-brand inputs and scale.  Internal design shops have deep product knowledge but fewer resources; agencies have creative muscle and tech-savvy teams.  In ranking, executives **buy**, marketing teams **use**, design teams **influence/validate**, and agencies are **power users/creators**.  

# Workflow & Job-to-be-Done Priorities  
Pharma asset creation involves many discrete steps.  We mapped the key “jobs” and assessed their frequency, pain, and fit for SwishX:  

- **Brief-to-Asset Creation (P0).**  Marketers start with a campaign brief or brand guide (audience, messaging, compliance rules).  Translating a brief into polished content is extremely common and labor-intensive.  For example, agencies typically build detailed scripts/storyboards for videos or animations from briefs, taking *weeks*.  Currently this is done manually (brainstorming, sketching).  AI can significantly accelerate it by generating compliant first-drafts: e.g. using structured prompts to ensure approved claims and safety language.  **Why P0:** High frequency (every campaign), high pain (long cycles, many revisions), high value (time saved).  SwishX should excel here by ingesting briefs (even as text or slides) and outputting draft images/videos.  

- **Create from Existing Source (P0).**  Often marketers supply raw content (data tables, scientific text, PowerPoint slides, logos).  Converting these into designs (infographics, explainer graphics, slide visuals or video storyboards) is very frequent.  It’s also high-pain: e.g. turning a whitepaper into a series of marketing slides or an infographic currently requires manual ideation and design.  AI tools excel at summarizing or visually representing text/numbers.  **Why P0:** Frequent use (repurposing MSL presentations, research data, training materials), high effort needed, and high business value (reinforces approved content).  SwishX should allow users to upload reference documents or images and auto-generate formatted assets.  

- **Create from Scratch (P1).**  Sometimes marketers or agencies develop entirely new concepts with no initial assets (e.g. a brand new campaign tagline or launch narrative).  Pure “blank canvas” creation is less common in pharma (most work is based on existing science/product info).  AI can brainstorm themes or sketch storyboards, but humans typically finalize direction.  **Why P1:** It’s useful (especially for brainstorming or concept testing), but lower priority than processing real source content.  SwishX could offer an “ideation mode” (e.g. moodboard-style image boards) as a secondary feature.  

- **Ideation & Creative Direction (P1).**  Related to above, this is the process of exploring concepts (e.g. mood boards, draft copy).  Currently done in meetings or slide decks.  It’s moderately painful (creative block, lack of ideas) and important for innovation.  AI can assist by suggesting creative prompts or sample visuals given a target message.  **Why P1:** Valuable for kickstarting campaigns but traditionally iterative.  SwishX should support it (e.g. “generate 5 visual concepts for [topic]”), but may not be the first killer feature.  

- **Transformation/Repurposing (P0).**  This covers workflows like “make a video from a slide deck” or “turn an infographic into a video.”  Essentially, converting content between formats.  Pharma teams have huge backlogs of content that must be repackaged across media.  It’s notoriously time-consuming.  For example, a video explaining a mechanism-of-action currently requires scripting, animation and review (often 4–8 weeks).  **Why P0:** Very high pain (multiple handoffs between PowerPoint, design, video editors) and high value (reusing data for HCP engagement).  SwishX can significantly speed this by automatically generating assets in a new format from an existing one (e.g. text-to-image infographics or slides-to-storyboard).  

- **Multi-Asset / Campaign Bundling (P0).**  Many campaigns require a coordinated set of assets (e.g. a series of social images, email banners, a short video, and a PDF brochure) all with consistent branding.  Today this means iterating on a base design manually for each format.  **Why P0:** High frequency for integrated campaigns, and huge pain (duplicative work across teams).  AI is well-suited to generate variations: for example, given one concept, produce multiple sizes and sub-variants.  SwishX should allow “generate image set” or “produce 3 poster sizes” in one step.  

- **Variations / Scaling / Localization (P0).**  Pharma companies market globally.  A single campaign often needs local language versions, or different content for multiple geographies or audiences.  Currently each market often redraws or manually relays content.  **Why P0:** Extremely high workload and waste.  Fortunately AI can auto-translate or recompose visuals for new languages and formats.  E.g. “Create US and EU versions of this poster” or “Generate region-specific charts.”  SwishX should make “mass variation” easy (and apply rules like local logo or disclaimers).  This aligns with reports that AI can produce “hundreds of content variations in minutes” when given structured prompts.  

- **Review / Judgment Support (P1).**  After assets are generated, marketers/designers and MLR reviewers must inspect them.  SwishX isn’t replacing Veeva, but it can assist.  For example, it can flag any deviations from brand guidelines, missing safety text, or out-of-date logos.  **Why P1:** Review is critical and a big bottleneck (“you can create a lot more content, but you still have to shepherd it through MLR”).  While SwishX need not manage approvals, embedding compliance cues into output (so “MLR eats it” more easily) is key.  Automated pre-checks (like verifying color spaces, required disclaimers) would add value, but full workflow optimization can come later.  

- **Refinement / Editing (P1).**  Once a draft asset exists, designers often make fine edits (layout tweaks, text edits, cropping).  Tools like Figma or Photoshop handle this today.  SwishX should export to those formats (e.g. layered PSD or Figma file) to let experts finalize designs.  The AI’s role is to minimize how much editing is needed.  **Why P1:** Important for user satisfaction, but more of a handoff/detail task.  Ensuring interoperability (e.g. SwishX images import cleanly) is critical.  

- **Reuse & Modular Content (P2).**  Reusing chunks of content (e.g. corporate logos, graphs, icons) is a known best practice.  Pharma teams use DAM systems (like PromoMats) for this.  SwishX can support reuse by remembering past assets or elements, but full DAM functionality is lower priority (we can integrate with Veeva instead).  **Why P2:** Valuable for efficiency but currently well-handled by existing systems.  We should ensure SwishX output is easily storeable/retrievable, but building a whole asset library is secondary.  

- **Advanced Control (P2).**  This covers deep design controls (e.g. exact color calibration, 3D camera moves, interactive timelines).  These are the domain of professional design tools and specialized roles (video editors, animators).  **Why P2:** Low frequency for typical marketing teams and very high complexity.  SwishX should avoid trying to replicate the full power of tools like After Effects on day 1.  Instead, output should be high-quality base content; advanced fine-tuning can be done outside SwishX as needed.  

In summary, **P0 priorities** are rapid generation from briefs/sources, bulk multi-asset creation, and scalable variation/localization – the core of what SwishX can automate.  **P1** includes ideation support, transformation between formats, and review assistance.  **P2** includes deep customization (beyond common layouts) and asset management, which we should *not* optimize initially.  

# Asset-Type Opportunity  
Pharma marketing uses a wide mix of media.  Key assets include static visuals, documents, and videos, each with different frequency and difficulty:  

- **Short-form Video (social ads, explainers) – P0:** Growing rapidly as pharma embraces digital.  High engagement with HCPs/patients but time-intensive to produce.  Agencies note that even a 60–90s pharma video can take 4–8 weeks of scripting, review and editing.  Because it’s so costly, there’s strong interest in automating it.  SwishX can differentiate here by generating animated clips or visual sequences from product data or charts (a domain with fewer off-the-shelf tools).  

- **Long-form Video (training, KOL presentations) – P0:** Many organizations invest in recorded talks, webinars and training videos.  These are extremely costly to produce (requiring set design, edits, narration).  While SwishX can’t replace filming real experts, it can help by generating animated supplements (e.g. MOA animations) or speeding up editing (e.g. creating draft cuts).  The potential value is very high, but so is complexity; focus first on bite-sized or animated segments.  

- **Infographics & One-Pagers – P0:** Highly valued in medical affairs and marketing.  They distill complex data into visuals.  Experts say “infographics are excellent and versatile tools for medical affairs teams” because they make data memorable.  However, creating them requires both design skill and domain understanding.  SwishX can target this by taking data inputs (charts, numbers, bullet points) and auto-generating infographic layouts.  Success here would be a clear win, as it directly addresses a known pain point.  

- **Static Images (banners, web graphics) – P1:** These include digital ad images, email headers, social media graphics.  They are produced frequently but are relatively simpler (often variations of logos/stock images with overlay text).  AI image generators already handle generic images quickly.  The challenge is styling them to brand.  SwishX should support this use case (e.g. “create LinkedIn banner” templates), but the ROI per asset is lower than multi-asset campaigns.  

- **Image Sets / Carousels – P1:** Multi-image campaigns (e.g. LinkedIn carousels, multi-panel prints) are common for storytelling or multi-point messaging.  They require consistency across images.  SwishX can help by taking one theme and producing a series of matched graphics.  This overlaps with “multi-asset creation” but specifically for still images.  (P1 priority because it’s important but often a subset of bigger campaigns.)  

- **Visual PDFs / Slide Decks – P1:** Sales and HCP materials often end as PowerPoint or PDF documents.  A slide deck (e.g. launch presentation) is central in pharma.  While SwishX isn’t a slide-builder, it can ease generation of slide visuals or canned PDF layouts from text/bullet inputs.  Many teams still rely on **Promotional Materials** like brochures and presentations – e.g. Veeva’s software explicitly handles “brochures, detail aids, presentations, [and] emails”.  AI could auto-populate these documents with approved content blocks.  This is useful (P1), but we’ll focus on generating the visual components rather than full decks.  

- **Scientific Illustrations & Animations – P2:** Complex 3D graphics (e.g. molecule models, mechanism-of-action animations) are very expensive and specialized.  They are high value (used in major conferences) but low volume.  Early SwishX shouldn’t attempt to replace dedicated 3D animation teams.  Instead, enabling simpler explainer graphics (2D animations or stylized 3D via templates) could be a future stretch goal.  Initially, we’ll consider these secondary (P2) and perhaps let agencies handle truly advanced visuals.  

- **Patient/KOL Story Content – P2:** Content like patient testimony videos or expert interviews are mostly shot in the real world.  AI may add value by generating subtitles or B-roll imagery, but creation of these is not core to SwishX’s immediate mission.  

**In summary:** Videos (especially short form) and infographics stand out as high-value, hard-to-produce, and ripe for AI assistance.  Static images and slide elements are important but more commoditized – SwishX can optimize by automating common creative tasks (branding, resizing) for them.  We should deprioritize ultra-specialized assets (advanced animations, live interviews) until core workflows are solved.  

# Tool & Platform Ecosystem  
Life sciences marketers work within a complex tech stack.  We mapped each main tool to user needs, comfort, and gaps SwishX must address or integrate with:  

- **Microsoft Office (PowerPoint/Word/Excel):** Universally used by marketing teams.  They’re trusted for text-driven content and slide decks (e.g. sales presentations).  Users are deeply familiar with them, so any AI output that ends up in slides or docs should align with Office’s formats.  (Indeed, job postings still require “expert PowerPoint” skills.)  *Opportunity:* SwishX can output PPT/Word-compatible content (e.g. charts, images) that marketers can drop into existing decks.  *Avoid:* Reinventing document layouts – stick to assets, not full document editing.  

- **Adobe Creative Suite:** (Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, Premiere, After Effects) – The industry-standard for professional design and video editing.  Design teams trust these for pixel-perfect control.  They are powerful but require training; non-designers find them complex.  (E.g. Veeva’s Head of Creative demands “expert Adobe” skills.)  *Opportunity:* SwishX should interoperate – export high-res, layered files that Adobe can import.  *Borrow:* The precision and flexibility of Photoshop/Illustrator (layers, masks) – e.g. provide editable layers.  *Avoid:* Building complex editing UIs; let designers use the tools they love for fine-tuning.  

- **Figma (and XD):** Rising in pharma content teams for UI and illustration.  It’s praised for real-time collaboration and version control.  (Veeva’s creative head explicitly prefers Figma alongside Adobe.)  *Opportunity:* Outputting to SVG or Figma files would ease handoff to design teams.  We should emulate Figma’s multi-user editability in SwishX’s review interface (e.g. multiple stakeholders annotating AI drafts).  

- **Canva (and similar web tools):** Gaining popularity for quick, templated graphics.  Non-designers love its simplicity.  However, in pharma it’s less entrenched (due to branding controls and lack of audit trail).  *Opportunity:* SwishX’s interface should be as user-friendly as Canva’s (“drag-and-drop” or fill-in templates) for non-experts.  *Avoid:* Restrictive complexity – favor simplicity.  

- **Veeva Vault/PromoMats:** The **standard** life-science asset repository and MLR workflow system.  Marketing teams heavily rely on it for tracking approvals and storing final assets.  Users find it functional and eventually user-friendly (one case study calls it “intuitive, easy-to-use”), but it’s not a creative tool.  Content must flow through Veeva for compliance.  *Opportunity:* SwishX should integrate with Veeva (e.g. output approved assets directly into PromoMats) and understand Veeva’s strict compliance rules.  *Borrow:* The auditability and global access model of Veeva (cloud-based, version control).  *Avoid:* Building our own standalone “vault” – defer to Veeva for final storage/approval.  

- **Collaboration & Workflow Tools:** Teams also use SharePoint, Teams/Slack, and Salesforce (for CRM/campaign tracking).  These tools are comfortable but not creative.  *Opportunity:* SwishX doesn’t replace them, but should fit into workflows (e.g. allow teams to start a SwishX creative request from a Teams chat or export results via SharePoint).  *Avoid:* Trying to replicate e-mail or chat; focus purely on content creation.  

- **Generative AI Tools:** Many marketers are already experimenting.  For example, pharma firms use image generators to quickly produce concept art, and agencies use chatbots to craft and normalize briefs.  These tools are popular for ad-hoc ideation, but they lack pharma-specific constraints (so outputs often fail MLR).  *Opportunity:* SwishX should leverage the advances of these AI engines (GPT-4, diffusion models, etc.) under the hood but wrap them in pharma-safe prompts and interfaces.  Users are curious about AI but weary of “hallucinations”, so SwishX must ensure outputs are factual/compliant.  *Borrow:* The instant “chat” or iterative UI mental model (e.g. allow users to tweak prompts).  *Avoid:* Uncontrolled open text generation – always tie outputs to verified content.  

In short, users **trust familiar software and workflows**.  They expect AI to slot into this ecosystem: SwishX should feel as intuitive as Canva or Figma, output standard file formats (PSD, PPT, MP4), and play nicely with Veeva and Teams.  We should emulate the ease-of-use of consumer tools while satisfying corporate requirements (audit trail, compliance).  Critically, SwishX won’t replace Photoshop or Veeva – it will complement them by doing the heavy creative lifting (like an always-on design intern) and then handing off to humans or existing systems.  

# SwishX Strategic Opportunity  
Synthesizing the above, the biggest opportunities are where SwishX can uniquely leverage AI to beat the status quo.  For each, we consider **frequency, pain, value, AI fit, differentiation, and complexity**:  

- **Automated Campaign Asset Generation (P0):**  *Opportunity:* Given a campaign brief or theme, auto-generate a suite of assets (images + a short video) aligned to brand/regulatory constraints.  *Rationale:* This tackles the most common, high-effort workflows.  Frequency is daily/weekly per team; current pain is enormous (agencies spend *weeks* prototyping each asset).  Business value is high – faster go-to-market and reduced agency spend.  GenAI is well-suited to patterns (e.g. turning bullet points into visuals).  Differentiation: train models on pharma-specific style and compliance (unlike generic DALL-E).  Complexity: medium (we need strong AI and user feedback loops), but the payoff justifies it.  **Focus:** SwishX should *obsess* on this – an interface where a marketer types (or uploads) their brief and gets ready-to-review visuals and video clips.  

- **High-Volume Variations & Localization (P0):**  *Opportunity:* Mass-generate language and format variants of any asset.  *Rationale:* Global pharma runs many similar campaigns across regions.  Frequency is high (each campaign often splits into 10+ local versions).  Manual translation/redesign is slow and error-prone.  AI can auto-translate text and even adjust imagery (e.g. change visual cues for local context).  Value: huge – elimination of repetitive work, faster international launches.  Differentiation: few tools handle on-brand automatic localization in pharma.  Complexity: moderate (needs multi-lingual, style-transfer).  **Focus:** Support “one-click translate or reskin” features for images/videos.  

- **Content Repurposing and Conversion (P0/P1):**  *Opportunity:* Transform existing content between formats (e.g. text→infographic, PPT→video).  *Rationale:* Media repurposing is constant: a medical paper might become a webinar, blog, and slide deck.  Frequency: very high, especially repackaging approved content for new channels.  Current pain: enormous, as it requires rethinking each asset.  AI advantage: strong (NLP + vision).  Value: high, because it multiplies the utility of each piece of IP.  Complexity: challenging (understanding semantics), but starting with simpler cases (like turning bullet points into a structured infographic) is feasible.  **Action:** Initially support text to graphics (infographic templates) and script-to-storyboard video conversion as “beta” use cases.  

- **Bulk Image & Video Creation for Scalability (P0):**  *Opportunity:* Given one concept, generate entire campaigns – image sets, multiple-length videos, different aspect ratios – at once.  *Rationale:* As noted, marketers need “dozens” of images and video cuts.  Pain: manual resizing, cropping, re-rendering is hugely time-consuming.  AI can produce multiple outputs in parallel.  Value: very high (instant multi-channel content).  Diff: existing tools can resize but not *re-compose* creatively.  Complexity: moderate.  **Action:** Build a “campaign builder” mode: input one prompt/artifact, output variations (e.g. square, vertical, horizontal; 30s, 15s clips; English/Spanish).  

- **AI-Guided Ideation & Brief Enhancement (P1):**  *Opportunity:* Assist users in writing better creative briefs and generating initial concepts.  *Rationale:* Agencies report using AI “to write better briefs” and align formats.  Pain: briefing is often vague.  AI can ask clarifying questions and suggest ideas.  Value: moderate but strategic (better briefs yield better outputs).  Diff: a true “smart prompt assistant” could stand out.  Complexity: moderate (requires NLP finesse).  **Focus after P0:** Integrate a chat/assistant UI that refines user input into structured prompts for asset creation.  

- **Compliance-Aware Content Checks (P1):**  *Opportunity:* Auto-flag potential compliance issues in AI drafts (missing mandatory text, prohibited words, color-usage guidelines).  *Rationale:* Pharma must comply with strict regulations.  Pain: reviewers currently catch avoidable errors late.  AI fit: good (text analysis, OCR).  Value: high (reduces revision cycles).  Complexity: medium (requires encoding complex rules).  **Approach:** Implement a “Pre-MLR review” feature that highlights where legal language should go or what images may be inappropriate.  

- **Integration with Brand/Template Libraries (P1):**  *Opportunity:* Leverage existing company assets (logos, style guides) when generating.  *Rationale:* Ensures brand consistency.  Pain: Without it, AI output could violate brand rules.  Value: necessary for adoption.  Complexity: low–moderate (import brand assets).  **Action:** Let teams import their style guides or have SwishX pull from Veeva’s approved asset library.  

**Lower priority / “not yet” (P2):** We should *not* attempt full campaign management or CRM integration (beyond basic handoffs) – stick to creation.  Similarly, avoid building very advanced editing (e.g. frame-by-frame video editing UI or complex 3D animation controls) in the MVP.  Those are better left to specialized platforms.  

**Implications for SwishX:** The product’s front-door should immediately present the core creation workflows (e.g. “New Image”, “New Video”, “Generate Variation”).  We should emphasize speed and simplicity: one-pagers or dashboards where users launch a generation job from a brief or file.  The interface should echo designers’ mental model (e.g. layers, brand assets panel) for trust, while also offering drag‑and‑drop and conversational prompt options for marketers.  The information architecture will likely center on “My Campaigns” (grouping related assets), “Templates/Guides” (imported brand rules), and an AI configuration panel (tone, language, region).  

Finally, we must validate all assumptions with real users.  Many of the above inferences (e.g. exactly which workflows are most painful) are based on industry reports and may vary by company.  Key unknowns include: which asset formats marketers will actually generate in SwishX vs. send to agencies, and how willing different personas are to trust AI outputs.  These uncertainties should be tested with interviews and prototypes as we move from research to product design.
